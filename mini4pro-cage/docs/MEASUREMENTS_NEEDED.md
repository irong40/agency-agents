# Measurements and files needed

These cannot be obtained from any source this build environment can reach. Calipers give the best results; a steel rule works for the long distances.

Set the drone on a flat table with the arms fully unfolded and the props installed. Turn one blade of each prop so it points straight outward, away from the fuselage.

| # | Measure | How | Parameter |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Prop diameter | Tip to tip across one prop, or the size printed on the blade | `prop_diameter` |
| M2 | Front motor to rear motor, same side | Centre of the prop screw to centre of the prop screw, straight line | `front_motor`, `rear_motor` x |
| M3 | Left front to right front motor | Centre to centre | `front_motor` y |
| M4 | Left rear to right rear motor | Centre to centre | `rear_motor` y |
| M5 | Prop hub height above table, front and rear | Table to the top face of the prop hub | rear z, `landing_z` |
| M6 | Arm width and height, front and rear | At a point about 20 mm from the motor centre, toward the body. Record that distance. | `*_arm_w`, `*_arm_h`, `*_clip_dist` |
| M7 | Arm centre height above table at that point | Table to the middle of the arm | `*_clip_dz` |
| M8 | Motor bell diameter | Widest part of the spinning bell | `motor_keepout_r` |
| M9 | Fuselage width at its widest | Across the body, excluding arms | `body_box` |
| M10 | Gimbal lens height above table and distance ahead of the front motors | | `camera_pt` |

## Photos that replace several measurements

1. Straight-down photo of the unfolded drone on a cutting mat or graph paper, with a ruler in frame. This gives arm angles and motor positions.
2. Side photo at table height, ruler in frame. This gives prop plane heights and arm heights.
3. Close-up of the underside of one front arm and one rear arm near the motor. This shows vents, wires, the landing foot and where a clip can sit.
